# The heap limit

Code: `crates/mini_racer_engine/src/process/platform.rs` (`set_heap_limit`, `read_sizing`, `SIZING`); `crates/mini_racer_engine/src/runner.rs` (`sized`, `recover`, `discard_microtasks`, `adopt`); `crates/mini_racer_engine/src/control.rs` (`HeapWatch`); `crates/mini_racer_engine/src/sys.rs` (`watch_heap_limit`, `near_heap_limit`); `ext/mini_racer_rs/src/heap_limit.rs`; `lib/mini_racer_rs/heap_limit_error.rb` (`MiniRacerRs::HeapLimitError`). Tests: `crates/mini_racer_engine/tests/heap_limit.rs`, `test/ruby/heap_limit_test.rb`, `test/ruby/fork/child_hits_the_heap_limit_of_a_context_it_takes_over_case.rb`, `test/ruby/fork/poisoned_context_stays_poisoned_in_child_case.rb`, `test/stress/heap_limit.rb`. Following a new V8's heap sizing: v8-bump step 15. An abort or an unearned `MiniRacerRs::HeapLimitError`: the debugging skill's quick reference.

`MiniRacerRs.heap_limit = bytes` (32 MiB through 4 GiB, `nil` for none) is read at each `Context.new`, so it holds for ExecJS's contexts too, and passed to V8 by `runner::sized`: `CreateParams::heap_limits(0, bytes)` splits it into a young generation of three semi-spaces of 2 to 32 MiB and the rest old, but V8 15.2 rounds a semi-space up to a power of two, which reserved up to a tenth more than the limit, so `sized` rounds it down and gives the old generation the rest, and V8 reserves the limit, less its rounding of the old generation down to a 256 KiB page. `heap_limits` also makes the code range the limit's size, on which V8 aborts the process unless it is a multiple of the allocation page (100,000,000 bytes did), so `sized` leaves the code range at V8's own size, as for a context without a limit; `MiniRacerRs.heap_limit` reads back the value set.

V8 calls the near-heap-limit callback (`sys::watch_heap_limit`, registered with the control block) when the old generation cannot grow, which it may do from a thread other than the runner, so the callback touches only `HeapWatch`'s atomics and the termination cell: it requests `HeapExhausted` for the armed dispatch, unless the runner is collecting on its own, and lends V8 room up to three times the old generation's limit at once, since V8 asks only around its last-resort collection and retries the allocation once before it aborts: lent half the limit at a time, a growing `Map`'s new backing store aborted at limits of 40, 80, 160 and 320 MiB. Past that room V8 aborts as it would without a limit: a `JSON.parse` whose result is about twice the limit did from 16 MiB down, and at 48, 96 and 192 MiB under `--minor-ms`.

V8 sizes the heap from `max_old_space_size`, `max_heap_size` or `max_semi_space_size` in place of the limit, and `predictable_gc_schedule` implies a semi-space larger than a small limit gives, so those flags and a limit refuse each other with `ArgumentError`. `read_sizing` follows `SetFlagsFromString` and fails closed: every argument of a flag up to `--` counts, `--no` forms, a value V8 reads from the next argument and a later argument V8 might never reach included, and only a flag that is one argument V8 reads whole (`--max_old_space_size=0`) clears an earlier one. Whatever it misses, a context V8 sizes past the limit is refused at `Context.new` with `ArgumentError`; `optimize_for_size` and `stress_compaction` imply a 1 MiB semi-space, which only shrinks the heap, and are taken.

After a dispatch in which V8 called the callback, `recover` collects in full and puts the limit back only when the live heap and a quarter fit under it, since V8's `RestoreHeapLimit` goes no lower than that; a heap that does not fit poisons the context, which then refuses every eval, call, microtask checkpoint and pump with its own message until disposed. It also collects after a script terminated with the heap that full, which V8 otherwise reports through the callback in the next script.

A script that hit the limit has its leftover microtasks dropped rather than drained: V8 empties the queue when a termination reaches it there. `HeapExhausted` is not a pending cause (`CauseSet::PENDING`): it ends with the dispatch it arrived in, as `recover` settles the heap then, so leftover microtasks of a stopped or timed-out script that reach the limit report it with that script, it outranking the stop or timeout, a microtask checkpoint whose microtasks reach it raises it itself, and one landing as a result serializes without stopping it is dropped.

A child that takes a capped context over registers the callback again with its own block (`runner::adopt`), as the inherited one names the parent's; removing the callback when none is registered is V8's `UNREACHABLE`. ArrayBuffer backing stores, shared sources and snapshot builds are outside the limit; `max_memory:` works as before beside it.
